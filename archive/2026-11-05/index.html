Part three: News

Today in tech

10 stories from 4 November 2026, each with a note on why it matters for Ben.

AI and LLMs

  1. Grok Bot can now make phone calls (opens in new tab)

    The official Grok Bot account said on Nov 4 that the bot can place calls for users: it shows a call card to approve, runs a live transcript, and lets the user take over or end the call. Coverage says it is limited to US numbers for now.

    Why it matters: Agents that act over voice, with an approval step and a live transcript, are exactly the agent-plus-human-in-the-loop pattern he builds and can point to in interviews.

    TeslaNorth, 4 Nov 2026

  2. Musk tightens Grok Bot usage limits, citing serving costs (opens in new tab)

    Elon Musk said the cost of serving Grok Bot was "unsustainably high relative to revenue" as subscribers reported weekly allowances running out early.

    Why it matters: Inference cost is now the constraint for agent products, so engineers who can make agents cheaper to run are in demand.

    Tech Times, 4 Nov 2026

  3. Anthropic prepares IPO as compute costs weigh (opens in new tab)

    TechCabal reports Anthropic is preparing an IPO, possibly as soon as Nov 16, after earning $17 billion in Q3 2026 revenue while spending about $10 billion on compute.

    Why it matters: The biggest AI IPO so far will shape funding and hiring across AI startups, including the ones he applies to.

    TechCabal, 4 Nov 2026

Startups and funding

  1. Fal raises $1.3B at a $16B valuation (opens in new tab)

    AI inference startup Fal raised a $1.3 billion Series F led by Bessemer, with Nvidia participating, doubling its valuation from $8 billion in April.

    Why it matters: Inference platforms are where much of the AI money is going, and hosted model APIs like Fal's are tools he can use in agent builds.

    Tech Startups, 4 Nov 2026

  2. Traversal raises $120M for AI agents that fix outages (opens in new tab)

    New York-based Traversal raised a $120 million Series B led by Kleiner Perkins, Redpoint and Khosla, bringing total funding to $173 million.

    Why it matters: Agents for site reliability are a growing niche, and the company says it will hire in research and engineering.

    AlleyWatch, 4 Nov 2026

  3. Lazarus AI and Guava raise for self-hosted and voice AI (opens in new tab)

    Boston-based Lazarus AI raised $43 million for AI systems organisations run under their own control, and Guava raised $12.5 million for enterprise voice AI.

    Why it matters: Self-hosted AI and voice agents both match his quantised-LLM and agent experience.

    Tech Startups, 4 Nov 2026

Hiring market

  1. Canva cuts 1,271 roles, about 22% of staff (opens in new tab)

    Canva is cutting or changing 22% of its workforce as growth slows and AI costs rise, merging product, growth and platform teams and moving hybrid staff back to the office part of the week.

    Why it matters: Even strong product companies are trimming and asking people to cover more ground, which favours generalist full-stack and AI engineers.

    TechCrunch, 4 Nov 2026

  2. Fiserv has cut 3,000 jobs; US openings rise to 7.52 million (opens in new tab)

    Fiserv said it has cut 3,000 jobs since June, while US job openings rose to 7.52 million in September, though hiring is at its slowest pace since 2013.

    Why it matters: Payments giants are cutting while overall hiring stays slow, so fewer seats open up for new graduates, which makes targeted applications matter more.

    Samwise Agency, 4 Nov 2026

Kenya fintech

  1. Nedbank completes takeover of 66% of NCBA (opens in new tab)

    South Africa's Nedbank completed its purchase of a 66% stake in NCBA, Kenya's fifth-largest bank, pushing NSE trading to KES 107.7 billion in a day.

    Why it matters: A new owner often brings technology and digital-banking changes, so NCBA may be one to watch for fintech engineering roles.

    TechCabal, 4 Nov 2026

  2. Safaricom says M-PESA created KES 478bn in social value (opens in new tab)

    Safaricom's sustainability report says M-PESA generated about KES 478.1 billion in social value in FY26 and that 57.8% of M-PESA transactions carried no customer charge.

    Why it matters: M-PESA remains the backbone of Kenyan fintech integrations, which is the kind of work he did on loans and payments.

    TechTrends Kenya, 4 Nov 2026
