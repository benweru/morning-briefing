Part three: News

Today in tech

9 stories from 15–16 November 2026, each with a note on why it matters for Ben.

AI and LLMs

  1. Meituan releases LongCat-2.5 weights, a 1.6-trillion-parameter agent model (opens in new tab)

    The mixture-of-experts model has about 48 billion active parameters per token, a 1-million-token API context and MIT-licensed weights; Meituan is charging a promotional price of about EUR 0.27 per million input tokens and EUR 1.07 per million output tokens.

    Why it matters: An open-weight, agent-oriented model with OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible APIs is another option for the automation work he builds.

    The Decoder, 15 Nov 2026

  2. Alibaba puts Qwen4-Max on its international Model Studio (opens in new tab)

    The 16 November changelog lists Qwen4-Max as a flagship multimodal model for coding, office work, frontend design and agent tasks, with web search, tool calling, structured outputs and OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible APIs.

    Why it matters: A new flagship with tool calling and familiar API formats is relevant to the LLM integrations he ships.

    Alibaba Cloud Model Studio, 16 Nov 2026

  3. Apple opens a US study app on how people use clinically grounded health AI (opens in new tab)

    In partnership with OpenEvidence, Apple aims for up to 200,000 US participants aged over 21 on iPhone 15 or later; answers are grounded in sources including NEJM, JAMA and Nature, data is not used to train models, and OpenEvidence deletes query and response data after 24 hours.

    Why it matters: It shows how a large consumer platform is testing health AI, even though the study itself is limited to the US.

    TechCrunch, 15 Nov 2026

Startups and funding

  1. Bharat Semi raises $24 million to build AI-enabled image sensors (opens in new tab)

    The all-equity seed round was led by Info Edge Group, South Park Commons and Indus Bridge Ventures; the pre-revenue New Delhi startup has about 75 employees, plans first commercial production in 2027, and has begun hiring engineers in Europe.

    Why it matters: A well-funded hardware-and-AI startup that is already hiring engineers outside India is a reminder of where specialised AI roles are opening.

    TechCrunch, 15 Nov 2026

Hiring market

  1. Australia's central bank: AI effects are showing up first among young software workers (opens in new tab)

    An RBA analysis finds job postings have fallen since ChatGPT launched in late 2022, with the steepest drop in the most AI-exposed occupations, and weaker employment among workers aged 15 to 24 and young graduates in that group; the article also cites an Anthropic review that junior technical hires fell from 27 percent to 9 percent since early 2025.

    Why it matters: Junior software hiring is under pressure in AI-exposed fields, which is the market he is applying into.

    The Decoder, 15 Nov 2026

Kenya fintech

  1. Safaricom is pushing M-Pesa beyond payments into a wider financial-services platform (opens in new tab)

    In the six months to 30 September 2026, M-Pesa revenue was KSh100.1 billion (42.8 percent of Safaricom revenue) and financial-services revenue was KSh7.1 billion, up 55.3 percent, with 17.5 million credit customers, 3.4 million money-market-fund customers and 67,500 insurance policies issued, up 852.2 percent.

    Why it matters: The payments and credit stack he already works around in Kenyan fintech is still expanding.

    TechTrendsKE, 15 Nov 2026

  2. Kenya weighs child social-media rules as Meta expands AI age checks (opens in new tab)

    The proposed Online Safety Bill 2026 would require parental consent and age verification; companies that fail to comply could face fines of up to Sh500 million or 10 percent of annual global turnover, whichever is higher.

    Why it matters: New platform rules in Kenya can change how consumer-tech and fintech products handle identity and age checks.

    Business Daily, 15 Nov 2026

  3. CBK and CMA tell virtual-asset providers to apply for licences (opens in new tab)

    A joint public notice dated 13 November 2026, reported on 15 November, requires anyone providing or intending to provide virtual-asset services in or from Kenya to apply under the Virtual Asset Service Providers Act, 2025; most categories carry a KSh100,000 application fee, and the regulator must decide within 30 days once due diligence is complete.

    Why it matters: Licensing for virtual-asset firms is a live compliance issue in the Kenyan fintech market.

    StartupNews Africa, 15 Nov 2026

  4. A Microsoft outage pushed Kenyan banks onto WhatsApp and phone lines (opens in new tab)

    The outage began around 4 PM GMT on Thursday and affected users in 74 countries; in Kenya at least six lenders sent notices, including Equity, Standard Chartered, Stanbic, UBA, I&M, Loop and NCBA's digital bank, while payments and mobile banking were reported as unaffected.

    Why it matters: It shows how Kenyan financial institutions still depend on a single cloud vendor for everyday operations.

    TechCabal, 15 Nov 2026
