Today in tech

10 stories from 15–16 November 2026, each with a note on why it matters for Ben.

AI and LLMs

  1. OpenAI begins embedded assessments with four independent labs (opens in new tab)

    On 16 November OpenAI said it had signed agreements with Apollo Research, METR, Transluce and Goodfire for its first phase of embedded assessments, giving them deep access to internal models and systems to review alignment and safeguards.

    Why it matters: Independent safety evaluations of frontier models are becoming a formal part of how labs work, which matters if he keeps building with those platforms.

    OpenAI, 16 Nov 2026

  2. Hugging Face routes language-model inference through OpenRouter (opens in new tab)

    On 16 November Hugging Face said Inference Providers now send language-model requests through OpenRouter, which picks a provider by live price, speed and reliability and retries on failure, with no change to existing Hugging Face tokens or code.

    Why it matters: He already works with LLM APIs and agents, so a single Hugging Face token that can route open-weight models across providers is a practical tooling change.

    Hugging Face blog, 16 Nov 2026

  3. OpenAI expands free ChatGPT access for academic researchers (opens in new tab)

    On 16 November OpenAI said it is expanding ChatGPT for Academic Researchers toward 200,000 researchers worldwide, with applications for 50,000 new ChatGPT Plus grants opening on 23 November, each covering the recipient and up to two collaborators.

    Why it matters: The programme is a reminder of how widely Plus-tier model access is being distributed to research teams, including possible collaborators.

    OpenAI, 16 Nov 2026

Startups and funding

  1. Corma raises $125 million Series A at a $1 billion valuation (opens in new tab)

    On 16 November TechCrunch reported that cybersecurity AI startup Corma raised a $125 million Series A at a $1 billion valuation led by NEA, three months after launch, bringing total funding to $185 million including Sequoia Capital, Khosla Ventures and Coatue.

    Why it matters: Fast-growing AI-agent companies in security are still raising large rounds, which is the kind of firm that later hires automation and backend engineers.

    TechCrunch, 16 Nov 2026

  2. Yoco secures a $100 million credit facility for merchant cash advances (opens in new tab)

    On 16 November Disrupt Africa reported that South African fintech Yoco secured a US$100 million credit facility from Victory Park Capital to scale merchant cash advances, after advancing more than ZAR6.5 billion (US$395 million) to over 40,000 merchants since 2018.

    Why it matters: African payments and merchant-credit platforms are still raising growth capital, which is close to the fintech work he already does.

    Disrupt Africa, 16 Nov 2026

  3. Kenyan startup Farmerlifeline wins a $50,000 Innovate Africa grant (opens in new tab)

    On 16 November Tech In Africa reported that Farmerlifeline Technologies, a Kenyan agritech startup, won the third Innovate Africa Challenge and a $50,000 implementation grant for solar-powered AI cameras that send SMS pest and disease alerts to farmers.

    Why it matters: A Kenyan AI-in-agriculture winner is a local company to watch as it runs a six-month field deployment.

    Tech In Africa, 16 Nov 2026

Hiring market

  1. US software developers aged 22 to 25 remain about a fifth below late 2022 levels (opens in new tab)

    A 15 November analysis of Stanford ADP payroll data, with figures to October 2026, found the employment index for US software developers aged 22 to 25 at 81.4, down almost a fifth since November 2022, while ages 41 to 49 stood at 120.2 and US developer job ads were 18% below February 2020.

    Why it matters: Junior hiring is still tighter than mid-career hiring in the US data, which is why worldwide and Africa-open roles remain the better targets.

    Datahub, 15 Nov 2026

Kenya fintech

  1. Safaricom says it is using AI against scams as M-PESA tops KES100 billion in half-year revenue (opens in new tab)

    On 16 November TechTrendsKE reported that Safaricom net income rose 35.9% to KES58.2 billion in the six months to September 2026, M-PESA passed KES100 billion in half-year revenue for the first time, and Group Chief Risk Officer Nicholas Mulila said the company is using AI to detect threats while pursuing AI certification by February.

    Why it matters: Kenya's largest fintech platform is investing in AI for fraud and security, which sits next to the payments and automation work on his CV.

    TechTrendsKE, 16 Nov 2026

  2. Safaricom is pushing M-PESA beyond payments into credit, savings and insurance (opens in new tab)

    On 15 November TechTrendsKE reported that in the six months to 30 September 2026 Safaricom recorded KSh7.1 billion in financial services revenue, up 55.3%, with 17.5 million credit customers, 3.4 million money market fund customers and 67,500 insurance policies, while M-PESA itself generated KSh100.1 billion, or 42.8% of total revenue.

    Why it matters: M-PESA is being run as a distribution platform for credit and insurance, which is useful context for Kenyan fintech roles.

    TechTrendsKE, 15 Nov 2026

  3. Safaricom says refugee IDs can be used to register for SIM and M-PESA (opens in new tab)

    On 16 November ECONEWS reported that Safaricom's 2026 Sustainable Business Report lists recognition of refugee identification documents for SIM and M-PESA registration in Dadaab, Kakuma-Kalobeyei and urban areas, without giving a registration total.

    Why it matters: Identity and onboarding rules in Kenyan mobile money are a recurring theme in local fintech work.

    ECONEWS, 16 Nov 2026
