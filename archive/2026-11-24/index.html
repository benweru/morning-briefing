Part three: News

Today in tech

11 stories from 23 November 2026, each with a note on why it matters for Ben.

AI and LLMs

  1. Anthropic launches Claude Ship to review, fix and approve pull requests (opens in new tab)

    Claude Ship has three parts: Code Review (generally available) and two beta agents, PR Steward (fixes failing CI, merge conflicts and review comments) and Claude Approvals (approves routine changes under a policy file kept in the repo); it is rolling out to Claude Team and Enterprise plans for GitHub repositories.

    Why it matters: Agent-driven code review and approval is becoming part of normal engineering workflows, which is the kind of tooling he builds and will be asked about.

    TechCrunch, 23 Nov 2026

  2. Strands releases Decider v2, five open models that pick tools and routes for agents (opens in new tab)

    Five Gemma 4-based models from E2B to 31B under Apache-2.0; the 31B averages 82.1 on six text benchmarks, up from 70.0 for v1 26B-A4B, and the post says an Amazon ticketing team cut cost 98% and latency 78% by using v1 instead of Claude Sonnet 4.6 for ticket labeling.

    Why it matters: Small open models that make routing and tool-choice decisions cheaply are a practical way to cut cost in agent projects.

    Strands Agents blog, 23 Nov 2026

  3. Gemini API adds file slicing so agents load only the pages or cells they need (opens in new tab)

    Developers can pick page ranges, sheet cell ranges and media segments of PDFs, Office files, audio and video, and tokens are billed only for the parts Gemini inspects; it works across Gemini 4 models and adds native upload of Microsoft Office and OpenDocument files.

    Why it matters: It lowers token cost for document-heavy agents without local PDF or ffmpeg tooling in the harness.

    Google blog, 23 Nov 2026

Startups and funding

  1. Sierra raises $1.75 billion at a $30 billion valuation (opens in new tab)

    The Series F was led by Greenoaks and Lightspeed and doubles the $15 billion valuation from under six months ago; Sierra says it serves almost 50% of the Fortune 50 and has doubled run-rate revenue to $400 million in five months.

    Why it matters: Customer-service agents are one of the best-funded areas of agentic AI, which means more hiring for people who build and integrate them.

    TechCrunch, 23 Nov 2026

  2. Supertake raises $20M seed for AI agents that turn market views into portfolios (opens in new tab)

    Adjacent led the round with Coinbase Ventures and USV; agents cannot deposit or withdraw without the user's consent and never see brokerage credentials, and the team plans to hire in engineering, design and growth.

    Why it matters: A fintech example of agents with hard guardrails and spend limits, close to his fintech work.

    TechCrunch, 23 Nov 2026

  3. Nairobi's Axmed gets $4.28M Gates Foundation grant for pharmacy procurement (opens in new tab)

    Of the total, $2.28 million launches Axmed Retail through a Family Planning Retail Demand Pool in Kenya, pooling orders across private pharmacies, and $2 million funds family planning procurement in Nigeria.

    Why it matters: A Kenyan technology company funded to build procurement and supply-chain software, a possible employer for backend and integrations work.

    Axmed, 23 Nov 2026

Hiring market

  1. Report: AI is squeezing entry-level hiring more than it is causing layoffs (opens in new tab)

    The RAISE US and Burning Glass Institute report says white-collar industries have 1.5 million fewer jobs than pre-pandemic trends implied, early-career opportunities in widely AI-using industries shrank 3-8% on average between 2021 and 2026, and office and administrative jobs fell by 780,000 from 2023 to 2025.

    Why it matters: It is US data, but it matches what he sees in junior postings that ask for experience, so showing shipped work matters more.

    Semafor, 23 Nov 2026

  2. NPR: employers pulling back from entry-level hiring where AI use is highest (opens in new tab)

    Burning Glass Institute president Matt Sigelman says employers in industries furthest ahead in adopting AI are increasingly looking for people who already have experience, so graduates are being asked to start their careers in the middle.

    Why it matters: It explains why many junior listings ask for experience and why concrete projects carry weight.

    NPR, 23 Nov 2026

Kenya fintech

  1. Pesalink reaches basic inclusivity level after adding till and paybill payments (opens in new tab)

    AfricaNenda's 2026 report puts Pesalink among 31 African instant payment systems at the minimum inclusivity level; the new person-to-business service lets consumers pay through banks, Saccos or mobile money using till numbers, paybills or QR codes.

    Why it matters: Bank-to-merchant instant payments competing with M-PESA's till and paybill is directly relevant to Kenyan payments integrations.

    Business Daily, 23 Nov 2026

  2. Former M-PESA chief Esther Waititu joins Visa as regional country manager (opens in new tab)

    She led M-PESA at Safaricom from February 2023 to July 2026 and is now Visa's senior vice-president and group country manager for Southern and Eastern Africa; Business Daily says M-PESA and Airtel Money have lost four top bosses to banks and payment networks in the last year.

    Why it matters: Card networks, banks and mobile money are moving into each other's markets, which shapes where fintech engineering jobs will appear.

    Business Daily, 23 Nov 2026

  3. Nigeria's Payaza gets a Central Bank of Kenya payment service provider licence (opens in new tab)

    The licence lets Payaza offer payment gateway services in Kenya directly, following a similar designation in Zambia last month.

    Why it matters: Another payments company building in Kenya, so more payment-gateway and integration roles may follow.

    BusinessPress, 23 Nov 2026
