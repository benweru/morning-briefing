Part three: News

Today in tech

11 stories from 2 November 2026, each with a note on why it matters for Ben.

AI and LLMs

  1. Musk confirms Grok Bot usage limits were tightened over cost (opens in new tab)

    Some users saw weekly usage jump from as low as 50% to 100% over Halloween weekend; Musk wrote that "the cost of serving Grok Bot was unsustainably high relative to revenue" and has not said how limits will work going forward.

    Why it matters: Agent tools are getting pricier and more rationed, so knowing how to work efficiently within usage limits is becoming a real skill for AI engineers.

    TechCrunch, 2 Nov 2026

  2. Anthropic launches message threads beta for Claude API agents (opens in new tab)

    Agents send the full conversation once, then only new messages plus the last reply ID; Anthropic says wait time on 500,000 to 1M token requests fell about 35%, with prices and rate limits unchanged and threads expiring five minutes after the last request.

    Why it matters: A practical API change for anyone building long-running coding or support agents, the kind of work Ben pitches in AI engineer applications.

    The Next Web, 2 Nov 2026

  3. Deep Principle releases Hydrogen-1.0, a 753B open-weight science model (opens in new tab)

    MIT-licensed post-train of Z.ai's GLM-5.3 with 1M-token context; self-reported 38.3% on FrontierScience-Research vs 33.3% for GPT-6-Astra; tested on 8 GPUs with SGLang behind an OpenAI-compatible API.

    Why it matters: Shows open weights moving into specialist domains, and the SGLang and quantized (NVFP4/MXFP4) serving setup lines up with Ben's vLLM and quantization experience.

    MarkTechPost, 2 Nov 2026

Startups and funding

  1. Trajectory raises $45M Series A for continual-learning post-training (opens in new tab)

    The 18-person startup raised $45 million at a $300 million valuation, led by Sequoia with Nvidia, Bessemer and Bain Capital Ventures; customers include Harvey, Rogo, Decagon, Mercor and Nvidia, and its platform is now publicly available.

    Why it matters: Post-training open models from feedback is close to Ben's LoRA fine-tuning work, and a freshly funded small team is likely to be hiring.

    TechCrunch, 2 Nov 2026

  2. OPay shelves its $304 million US IPO a day after pricing (opens in new tab)

    OPay priced its ADS at $19 but trading never started; it withdrew the NYSE listing, citing a regulatory inquiry in an undisclosed foreign jurisdiction, leaving a planned $4 billion valuation in limbo.

    Why it matters: A big signal on how hard it is for African fintechs to reach public markets, which shapes funding and hiring across the sector Ben works in.

    TechCabal, 2 Nov 2026

  3. Nairobi took 41% of African tech funding in the first nine months of 2026 (opens in new tab)

    Tracxn says African tech raised $684 million in 9M 2026, up 30% from $527 million; Nairobi raised $277 million (41% of all funding), with Spiro the largest contributor; no new African unicorns this year.

    Why it matters: More capital landing in Nairobi means more local startups with money to hire engineers, though much of it sits with one company.

    Forbes Africa, 2 Nov 2026

Hiring market

  1. Squarespace cuts 19% of staff, mostly product and engineering (opens in new tab)

    About 300 jobs go; the CEO memo cites "smaller teams, more player coaches" and offers at least 16 weeks of base salary plus benefits through May 2027, as vibe-coding site builders reshape the market.

    Why it matters: Another sign that AI is shrinking traditional web engineering teams, so Ben's AI and agent skills matter more than plain front-end work.

    TechCrunch, 2 Nov 2026

  2. SUSE to cut about 10% of its workforce, citing automation and AI (opens in new tab)

    The CEO memo to SUSE's 1,900 staff says "automation and AI are fundamentally shifting how software companies operate"; affected staff will be told by November 4.

    Why it matters: Open-source infrastructure companies are also trimming roles AI can cover, which means more competition for remote jobs from experienced engineers.

    The Register, 2 Nov 2026

Kenya fintech

  1. Airtel Kenya buys 100MHz of 5G spectrum for KES 8.4 billion (opens in new tab)

    Airtel got 25-year rights to 100MHz in the 3,500MHz band (about US$65 million); Kenya's 5G subscriptions reached 2.1 million in June 2026, up 75% from 1.2 million a year earlier.

    Why it matters: Stronger competition with Safaricom on data could mean cheaper, faster connectivity for the mobile products Kenyan developers build.

    ITWeb Africa, 2 Nov 2026

  2. KRA chair defends a 25% phone tax at network activation, though it is not law (opens in new tab)

    The 25% activation tax was dropped from the Finance Act 2026, so the existing 10% excise duty on phones stays; KRA's chair argued import checks are beaten by under-declaration in consolidated cargo.

    Why it matters: Phone prices drive mobile and M-PESA adoption in Kenya, and the idea raises IMEI privacy questions relevant to anyone building local fintech.

    Techweez, 2 Nov 2026

  3. Kenswitch partners with PayU's Wibmo on 3-D Secure online card checks (opens in new tab)

    Banks connected to Kenya's domestic payment switch get an EMV 3-D Secure Access Control Server, with risk-based checks using OTPs, biometrics or push approvals, plus stronger domestic e-commerce processing.

    Why it matters: Better local card authentication makes it easier for Kenyan web apps to take online card payments alongside M-PESA.

    Business Standard (ANI press release), 2 Nov 2026
