Today in tech

10 stories from 7–9 November 2026, each with a note on why it matters for Ben.

AI and LLMs

  1. xAI turns on default text watermarking for Grok API models (opens in new tab)

    As of Nov. 7, SpaceXSI watermarks text by default from Grok 4.7, 4.6, 4.5, 4.3 and the Grok 4.20 models, including structured output and tool-call arguments in the Responses and Chat Completions APIs; look for `x-watermark: true` in response headers. Team admins can turn it off for eligible models in the Console if they confirm a legal exception applies.

    Why it matters: If he builds on the Grok API, tool-call arguments and structured JSON now carry a watermark header, and admins can switch it off per model.

    The Grid Fin, 8 Nov 2026

  2. Moonshot publishes Kimi K3.1 Pro weights; Tencent releases DSpine draft weights for Qwen3.8-27B (opens in new tab)

    Kimi K3.1 Pro climbed from 12th to 5th on OpenRouter (up 56.4%) after Moonshot published the weights. Tencent’s DSpine draft model reached 3.84 times ordinary autoregressive decoding throughput in its eight-request concurrent Office replay test, under Apache 2.0.

    Why it matters: Open-weight models are closing the gap, which helps anyone doing self-hosted inference or quantization like his capstone work.

    Daily Rundown (DIT), 8 Nov 2026

Startups and funding

  1. Anthropic still targeting a pre-Thanksgiving US IPO (opens in new tab)

    Targeting an $80 billion to $100 billion raise at a valuation of $1.25 trillion to $1.75 trillion; Q3 revenue rose nearly 50% to about $17 billion and the annualized run rate is about $81 billion. JPMorgan is expected to hold an investor briefing on Friday, Nov. 13.

    Why it matters: A listing of that size would shape AI hiring and compute spending across the industry.

    TokenPost, 8 Nov 2026

  2. DayOne files for US IPO while Groq seeks $8 billion valuation (opens in new tab)

    Singapore-based DayOne Data Centers submitted paperwork for a US IPO and is weighing a raise of about $5 billion. Nvidia-backed Groq is in talks on a Series B at an $8 billion valuation, more than double its August value.

    Why it matters: AI inference and data-centre capital keeps flowing, which supports demand for engineers who can build on top of it.

    Venturist, 8 Nov 2026

  3. Fal raises $1.65B and Salesforce agrees to buy Cresta for $2.7B (opens in new tab)

    Fal raised a combined $1.65 billion across Series E and F (the $1.3 billion F led by Bessemer Venture Partners), doubling its value since April to $16 billion. Salesforce agreed to acquire Cresta, a voice-first customer-experience platform, for $2.7B.

    Why it matters: Agentic and generative-media companies are raising large rounds, a sign of where AI engineering jobs are opening.

    Crowdfund Insider, 8 Nov 2026

Kenya fintech

  1. Kenya’s digital lenders face rules on AI credit decisions (opens in new tab)

    Regulation 60 of the CBK Non-Deposit Taking Credit Providers Regulations, 2026 (Legal Notice No. 191, gazetted Sept 29) requires providers to explain automated decisions, disclose AI interactions and keep human oversight; CBK had licensed 281 digital credit providers by September 2026.

    Why it matters: Kenyan fintechs using AI for credit scoring now need explainability and human review, which is relevant to loan-automation work like his.

    TechTrendsKE, 7 Nov 2026

  2. Nigeria’s Redtech plans a Kenya-Nigeria payments corridor (opens in new tab)

    Redtech’s CEO says its first Kenyan product launches by Q1 2027 at the latest, starting with payment and collection tools for micro, small and medium businesses; it has not named Kenyan partners or disclosed its CBK licensing status.

    Why it matters: A new cross-border payments entrant could mean API and integration roles in Nairobi from 2027.

    Techweez, 7 Nov 2026

  3. TechCabal: Kenyan banks keep posting record profits as Fuliza grows (opens in new tab)

    Combined pre-tax profits of Kenya’s largest lenders rose 17.6% to KES 305.95 billion in 2025; Fuliza revenue rose 86% to KES 4.9 billion in the six months to September 2026, with 17.2 million borrowers and an average advance down 24.6% to KES 192.

    Why it matters: Lending through M-PESA is where the growth is, which is the space his loan-simulation work sits in.

    TechCabal, 8 Nov 2026
