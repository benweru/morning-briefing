Today in tech

12 stories from 23–24 November 2026, each with a note on why it matters for Ben.

AI and LLMs

  1. OpenAI and Cisco release Shirudo, an open-source runtime that shields running Linux systems (opens in new tab)

    In a controlled test, a Codex-generated shield blocked a Java4Shell exploit attempt against a running Elasticsearch service without a restart; Shirudo uses eBPF, and operators approve a shield before it is deployed.

    Why it matters: Shows Codex-generated, human-approved fixes being applied to live systems, a pattern he can reuse in agent workflows with an approval step.

    OpenAI, 23 Nov 2026

  2. Google DeepMind launches Humanity's First Exam to test how reliably LLMs answer everyday questions (opens in new tab)

    The benchmark has 544 elementary text and image prompts across 20 foundational capabilities, measures whether models answer correctly every time rather than once, and comes with a Kaggle competition running through December 15.

    Why it matters: Reliability across repeated runs is what matters when he puts an LLM inside a product, and this gives a public way to measure it.

    Google blog, 24 Nov 2026

  3. OpenAI says GPT-6 models can design proteins using only reasoning and code (opens in new tab)

    In lab tests with Onava, proteins based on GPT-6.2's designs showed evidence of binding across five targets, and redesigned antibodies retained binding; an internal research model matched or outperformed specialized models on many design tasks.

    Why it matters: A signal that general-purpose models using code as a tool are replacing specialist models in new fields, which is how agent tooling is heading.

    OpenAI, 24 Nov 2026

Startups and funding

  1. South African founder's Lupa Robotics raises $1.67 million seed to teach robots human dexterity (opens in new tab)

    Long Journey Ventures led the round; the company will use the money to hire engineers, develop tactile sensing and data-collection systems, and build human-manipulation datasets.

    Why it matters: An African-founded AI company that is hiring engineers, and a sign investors are funding applied AI beyond chatbots.

    TechCabal, 23 Nov 2026

  2. AidenAI raises $32 million Series A to build and deploy enterprise AI agents (opens in new tab)

    A91 Partners led the round; the company says it plans a team of more than 1,000 frontier AI engineers through its AidenAI Builder Academy in Princeton and Hyderabad, and serves over 30 enterprise clients.

    Why it matters: An agent-building company planning to hire AI engineers in large numbers, in the area where he has built pipelines.

    PR Newswire, 24 Nov 2026

  3. Ajira AI brings job matching, CV tailoring and application autofill into one platform (opens in new tab)

    Founded by Gideon Kiratu, a former Senior Software Engineer at Microsoft, the platform combines job discovery, CV tailoring, cover letter generation, skills matching and help filling recruitment forms.

    Why it matters: A Kenyan product built around the exact job-search workflow he is going through, and an example of a local AI startup.

    Techweez, 23 Nov 2026

Hiring market

  1. Japan survey: 19.6% of firms recruiting graduates for clerical jobs plan to hire fewer as AI spreads (opens in new tab)

    Mynavi surveyed 1,667 companies; 10.3% of those hiring graduates for IT jobs are considering hiring more, with demand strongest among manufacturers adopting AI.

    Why it matters: AI is cutting routine entry-level roles while IT hiring holds up, which favours graduates who can show technical work.

    Kyodo News, 24 Nov 2026

  2. Microsoft expands early-career programs while offering buyouts to about 60,000 employees (opens in new tab)

    The Wire cites Revelio Labs data that entry-level postings fell 35% between 2023 and 2026, and says Microsoft's Aspire program had 2,100 global participants this year.

    Why it matters: Even as entry-level postings shrink, big employers are still investing in AI-fluent early-career talent.

    Workforce Observer, 24 Nov 2026

  3. Nigeria's data regulator targets a 500,000-job data protection pipeline (opens in new tab)

    The NDPC says the ecosystem has created more than 27,000 jobs in under three years and that Nigeria has over 10,000 certified data protection officers, and it wants to export compliance talent.

    Why it matters: Privacy and compliance skills are in demand across African fintech, and data protection work is a possible remote-friendly niche.

    TechCabal, 24 Nov 2026

Kenya fintech

  1. Safaricom says 3.7 million customers have opted into M-PESA Shiriki Pay (opens in new tab)

    Shiriki Pay lets a sponsor give up to five beneficiaries access to their M-PESA wallet, each with a monthly limit of up to KES 20,000; beneficiaries cannot withdraw cash or see the balance or history.

    Why it matters: A concrete delegated-spending feature on M-PESA, relevant to wallet permission and limit design.

    Safaricom, 24 Nov 2026

  2. Safaricom says AI helps prevent 94% of fraud attempts before they occur (opens in new tab)

    The company uses AI for fraud detection, credit scoring and network planning; its annual report says Fintech 2.0 raised M-PESA capacity from about 4,500 to 6,000 transactions per second, scalable to 10,000.

    Why it matters: Shows how fraud detection and lending models sit inside Kenya's biggest payments platform, close to the work he has done on loan pipelines.

    People Daily, 24 Nov 2026

  3. Kenya's eCitizen platform named in an unverified data breach allegation (opens in new tab)

    VECERT claimed on X that about 740 GB was taken, including roughly 600 GB of KYC documents and 40 GB of source code; Techweez says no public confirmation of the breach was found.

    Why it matters: A reminder of why file-upload validation and secure authentication matter in government and fintech systems.

    Techweez, 24 Nov 2026
