Part three: News

Today in tech

11 stories from 20–22 November 2026, each with a note on why it matters for Ben.

AI and LLMs

  1. Sarvam releases open-weight Sarvam-105B-1711 for agents and Indian-language voice (opens in new tab)

    The new open-weight 105B checkpoint averages 73.9 across eight conversational agent benchmarks (up from 42.6) at a blended $0.15 per million tokens, down from $0.44; the 65 GB NVFP4 version runs on a single workstation GPU, and BF16 weights are on Hugging Face for fine-tuning.

    Why it matters: A cheap open model with strong tool use that you can fine-tune yourself is a practical base for low-cost agent and voice projects.

    Sarvam AI, 22 Nov 2026

  2. Google previews Tool Retrieval in the Gemini API for large tool libraries (opens in new tab)

    With defer_loading set to true, the model sees only each tool's name and short description and loads a full schema when needed; server-side search, schema load and function call resolve in 1 API request, there is no separate fee, and Google advises static loading under 20 stable tools.

    Why it matters: If you wire agents to dozens of functions or MCP servers, this cuts the token cost of tool definitions, which is a common pain point.

    MarkTechPost, 22 Nov 2026

  3. Google announces Agent Compute Engine, microVM sandboxes that keep credentials outside the agent (opens in new tab)

    The managed service runs agents in isolated microVMs with checkpoint and resume, and an external Agent Gateway injects secrets so credentials never sit inside the sandbox; it is not yet available, pricing is unannounced, and Google targets a preview in the coming months.

    Why it matters: Sandboxing agents and keeping secrets out of them is becoming standard practice, and worth knowing for any agent work you pitch.

    InfoQ, 22 Nov 2026

  4. Signal limits screenshots on mobile to block OS-level AI agents from reading chats (opens in new tab)

    Signal's president Meredith Whittaker called operating systems that integrate agentic AI an 'existential threat' to its privacy guarantees; screenshot restrictions are added on Android and iOS, users can turn the setting off, and she admitted the fix is 'incomplete'.

    Why it matters: As you build agents that read screens and apps, expect apps and users to push back on that access, so design permissions in from the start.

    TechCrunch, 21 Nov 2026

Startups and funding

  1. Kenyan startup funding falls to a six-year low, per this week's African tech roundup (opens in new tab)

    Kenyan ventures raised US$201 million in the nine months to September 2026 versus US$744 million a year earlier (down 73%), and only 45 Kenyan startups raised US$100,000 or more (down 32%), according to Africa: The Big Deal figures reported by Business Daily.

    Why it matters: Tighter local funding means fewer and pickier startup hiring rounds, so lead with shipped, revenue-relevant work when you apply.

    Hackhouse, 22 Nov 2026

  2. Storspay expands B2B cross-border payroll to Kenya and five other African markets (opens in new tab)

    The Canada-based platform now covers Nigeria, Kenya, Uganda, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire and Benin, paying staff and suppliers in local currency to bank accounts and mobile money wallets; it advertises rates from 1% and commonly 2% on African corridors and has registered with FINTRAC.

    Why it matters: Cross-border payroll and payout APIs into Kenyan rails are a growing product area you could build for, and a possible employer type.

    Techweez, 22 Nov 2026

  3. Bending Spoons buys Mailchimp from Intuit for $3.68 billion (opens in new tab)

    Intuit paid roughly $12 billion for Mailchimp in 2021 and is taking a loss; the deal is about 90% cash and 10% stock ($368 million in new Bending Spoons shares), and Intuit says it will sharpen its focus on AI tools for small businesses.

    Why it matters: It shows how AI pressure on SaaS is reshaping product companies, and Bending Spoons-style acquirers consolidate engineering, which affects hiring.

    TechCrunch, 20 Nov 2026

Hiring market

  1. Enphase cuts about 320 jobs, 12% of staff, as it scales AI and automation (opens in new tab)

    In an SEC filing dated November 20, Enphase said it will reduce headcount by approximately 12% (about 320 employees), move certain functions to cost-efficient regions, and scale productivity through artificial intelligence and automation, with about $25 million to $28 million in restructuring charges.

    Why it matters: Another employer pairing cuts with offshoring and AI productivity, which can favour cost-efficient remote talent like you if you show AI-assisted delivery.

    SEC (Enphase Form 8-K), 20 Nov 2026

Kenya fintech

  1. Safaricom confirms a technical fault disrupting Ziidi logins and withdrawals (opens in new tab)

    Safaricom said on Saturday 21 November that a technical hitch was affecting Ziidi, its M-PESA money market fund, with no restoration time or cause given; Ziidi's assets under management rose 82.9% to KSh23.36 billion at half-year, and new Chief Financial Services Officer Boniface Mungania took permanent charge on 1 November.

    Why it matters: A live example of reliability risk in M-PESA-linked products, relevant to the M-PESA and wallet systems you build and test.

    Techpoint Africa, 21 Nov 2026

  2. Safaricom rebuts viral Ksh10 M-PESA reversal scam claim, points to Hakikisha (opens in new tab)

    Responding to a claim that fraudsters send small amounts like Ksh10 then reverse to drain a balance, Safaricom said reversal applies only to the amount sent or received; it says final status is communicated within two hours and urges use of Hakikisha, which shows the recipient's name before a payment.

    Why it matters: Fraud-proofing flows such as recipient confirmation and scoped reversals are core design points for any M-PESA integration.

    People Daily, 21 Nov 2026

  3. Top Kenyan banks ask CBK for a 12-month pause on new capital rules (opens in new tab)

    Bank chief executives want CBK's prudential guidelines for systemically important banks, due 1 January 2027 with extra buffers of 0.5 to 2.5 percent of the loan book, delayed a year; private-sector credit growth hit 10.6 percent in September 2026 versus a 2.9 percent contraction in January.

    Why it matters: Bank capital rules and the 2027 election year shape lending and fintech partnerships, so they affect where Kenyan fintech hiring goes.

    Business Daily, 22 Nov 2026
