Today in tech

10 stories from 12–13 October 2026, each with a note on why it matters for Ben.

AI and LLMs

  1. Google ships Gemini 4 Argon to developers (opens in new tab)

    Gemini 4 Argon is out globally via the Gemini API in AI Studio at an introductory $2/$10 per million input/output tokens (rising to $4/$20 after Nov 30, 2026), with a 1M unified input-output token limit; Google says it lifted agentic code submissions in Antigravity by more than 35%.

    Why it matters: A new frontier model priced for API builders is directly usable in the LLM pipelines and agents he builds.

    Google (The Keyword), 13 Oct 2026

  2. Liquid AI releases open-weight LFM2.5-24B-A2B for on-device agents (opens in new tab)

    The 24B-parameter model uses 2B active parameters, has a 131,072-token context, scores 27.69 on AutomationBench versus 8.72 for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, and ships open-weight on Hugging Face with API pricing of $0.10/$1.00 per million input/output tokens.

    Why it matters: A cheap open model tuned for tool calling and automation fits n8n-style workflows that need to run locally or at low cost.

    Liquid AI blog, 13 Oct 2026

Startups and funding

  1. Nairobi founder's Startup Map Hub helps founders find and apply for funding (opens in new tab)

    Launched in October 2025 by Nairobi software engineer Duncan Muriithi, Startup Map Hub has 2,415 registered founders, at least 35 paying, and over $2,000 in revenue since it began charging in February 2026.

    Why it matters: A small Nairobi-built AI-assisted product, the kind of early-stage local team that hires generalist engineers.

    TechCabal, 13 Oct 2026

  2. African VCs say AI startups need moats beyond the model (opens in new tab)

    Investors from TLcom, Madica, Village Capital and others say they now pay for local-language voice, deep customer integration, licences and revenue; TechCabal Insights counted 207 African AI startups across 17 countries in 2025, up from 104 in 2022.

    Why it matters: Shows where African AI money is going: integration-heavy products, which matches his integrations-and-LLM profile.

    TechCabal, 12 Oct 2026

  3. Infinite Partners closes $169.3 million South African fund (opens in new tab)

    The mid-market private equity firm raised R2.8 billion ($169.3 million) for Core Equity Fund II, above its R2.5 billion target, with first investments in enterprise software firm e4 and digital infrastructure business CYNK.

    Why it matters: More capital for tech-enabled African businesses such as enterprise software can mean more engineering hiring across the region.

    TechCabal, 12 Oct 2026

Hiring market

    Why it matters: Hundreds of experienced Nairobi engineers may enter the local job market at once, raising competition for the roles he is targeting.

  2. Bending Spoons lays off Airtable staff a month after buying it (opens in new tab)

    Bending Spoons says the layoffs include a "large part" of the Airtable team, one month after the acquisition.

    Why it matters: Acquisition-driven cuts keep adding experienced engineers to the remote job pool he is competing in.

    The Verge, 13 Oct 2026

Kenya fintech

  1. CBK seeks powers to take over struggling payment firms (opens in new tab)

    The draft National Payment System Bill, 2026 would let the Central Bank of Kenya intervene in a payment provider that fails its obligations to customers, appoint a statutory manager to sell the business and remove officials it holds responsible.

    Why it matters: Tighter oversight of Kenyan payment fintechs shapes how firms like Kwara build compliance into their products.

    TechCabal, 12 Oct 2026

  2. Safaricom launches Ziidi Shari'ah fund inside M-PESA (opens in new tab)

    The Shari'ah-compliant money market fund, run with GulfCap Investment Bank and regulated by the CMA, allows free deposits and withdrawals from M-PESA, subject to the KES 250,000 per-transaction and KES 500,000 daily limits.

    Why it matters: M-PESA keeps moving into savings and investing, a growing area for Kenyan fintech engineering work.

    tech.africa, 13 Oct 2026

  3. Safaricom says M-PESA limits are set by CBK (opens in new tab)

    Replying to a customer's call for a Ksh10 million limit, Safaricom said on X that mobile money limits are regulated by CBK; the limits stay at Ksh250,000 per transaction, Ksh500,000 a day and Ksh500,000 account balance.

    Why it matters: Transaction caps are a constraint any M-PESA integration he builds has to handle.

    People Daily, 13 Oct 2026
