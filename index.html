Today in tech

11 stories from 19 October 2026, each with a note on why it matters for Ben.

AI and LLMs

  1. Nvidia ships open Nemotron 3.5 Super VL and Bolt models for agents (opens in new tab)

    Nemotron 3.5 Super VL is a 120-billion-parameter open-weight model for enterprise agents, and two lightweight Bolt models run locally on RTX Spark AI PCs and DGX Spark workstations; Nvidia names Ramp, EY, Perplexity and Salesforce as users.

    Why it matters: Free, open models he can run or fine-tune for agent and automation work instead of paying per token.

    TechCrunch, 19 Oct 2026

  2. MiniMax M3.1 Flash cuts agent costs by 70% (opens in new tab)

    MiniMax says M3.1 Flash makes inference nearly three times faster and agent-task usage 70% cheaper than its predecessor, at $0.15 input and $0.60 output per 1M tokens; it's on the MiniMax API now, with weights to follow later.

    Why it matters: A very cheap model for the high-volume agent pipelines he builds.

    MiniMax, 19 Oct 2026

Startups and funding

  1. Tenstorrent raises $1.5 billion at a $6.2 billion valuation (opens in new tab)

    The inference-chip startup led by Jim Keller raised $1.5 billion, more than doubling its $2.6 billion late-2024 valuation, in a round led by Trace Capital and Innovation X.

    Why it matters: The week's biggest AI funding round, and a sign that money for AI infrastructure is still flowing.

    TechCrunch, 19 Oct 2026

  2. AI assistant startup Town becomes a unicorn four months after launch (opens in new tab)

    Town, founded by former Plaid CTO Jean-Denis Greze, raised a $108 million Series B co-led by Forerunner and Menlo Ventures, taking total funding to at least $160 million.

    Why it matters: Workplace AI assistants that plug into email and other tools are the kind of integration work he does.

    TechCrunch, 19 Oct 2026

  3. Onafriq tried to sell its Nigerian agent-banking arm Baxi before shutting it (opens in new tab)

    Onafriq announced Baxi's closure on October 16, citing inflation, naira depreciation and the shift to account-to-account transfers; its agency revenue fell 56% to $13.4 million in 2024.

    Why it matters: Shows how fast African fintech business lines change as payments move to direct transfers.

    TechCabal, 19 Oct 2026

  4. Five35 Ventures makes 3.5x on its Complete Farmer exit (opens in new tab)

    The gender-focused VC sold its whole stake in Ghanaian agritech Complete Farmer in March 2026 for 3.5 times its money, a 38% IRR, as the company's valuation rose from $6 million to $49 million.

    Why it matters: Rare African venture exits help keep funding flowing to startups that hire engineers.

    TechCabal, 19 Oct 2026

Hiring market

  1. Microsoft's Nairobi engineering centre closure cuts more than 400 jobs (opens in new tab)

    TechCabal reports Microsoft has shut its Africa Development Centre in Nairobi, leaving more than 400 engineers without jobs, after closing its Lagos hub in May 2024; about five staff will remain in the affected operation.

    Why it matters: Hundreds of experienced Nairobi engineers are now on the local job market, which raises competition for the roles he's applying to.

    TechCabal, 19 Oct 2026

  2. Anthropic study: 80% of Claude session time is agents waiting on humans (opens in new tab)

    Anthropic's study of its own staff found 80% of elapsed time in Claude sessions was agents waiting on human input, and the median employee spends 20% more time in meetings.

    Why it matters: Managing several AI agents at once is becoming the job, and that's a skill he can show.

    Fortune, 19 Oct 2026

  3. monday.com nudges middle managers out in its AI reorganisation (opens in new tab)

    After laying off 350 people (20% of staff) this summer, monday.com is steering managers to resign; five senior executives, including two R&D VPs, are leaving or about to leave.

    Why it matters: AI is flattening software teams, so hands-on builders are in a better spot than managers.

    Ctech (Calcalist), 19 Oct 2026

Kenya fintech

  1. Safaricom launches M-PESA Jumbe to hide customer details on payment alerts (opens in new tab)

    Jumbe replaces a customer's name and number with a JumbeID such as NK_74, and lets Lipa na M-PESA and Pochi la Biashara merchants add up to 50 staff to receive payment notifications.

    Why it matters: A new M-PESA notification flow that wallet and merchant apps like his X Money project may need to handle.

    Capital FM, 19 Oct 2026

  2. Pesapal and Drift Consult take the RACK platform to retail shops (opens in new tab)

    Pesapal and Drift Consult partnered to drive adoption of RACK, which combines payments, sales and business management for retail and hospitality businesses; Drift Consult will help firms digitise their operations.

    Why it matters: Kenyan payments companies are bundling software with payments, which creates integration work.

    Pesapal, 19 Oct 2026
